CH. 01

The Silence Before the Tick

Every watch we finish begins in a room with no clock in it. The watchmaker arrives before dawn, warms the oil between thumb and forefinger, and listens to the nothing — because a movement is only ever as honest as the silence it interrupts.

Our founder carved her first escape wheel from a broken loom part. She called the finished watch Pièce Zéro, and swore it would never be sold. It hasn't been. It keeps time to this day, badly, beautifully.

"We do not sell time. We sell the small ceremony of consulting it."
CH. 02

Calibre 016 — The Patient Heart

Two hundred and sixteen components, each finished by hand under 40× magnification: bevels drawn to mirror, screws blued over open flame to the exact second the color turns cornflower, then pulled back.

The balance breathes 28,800 times an hour. You will never hear it. That is the point — luxury is a machine so refined it becomes geology: silent, certain, older than its owner by design.

CH. 03

The Guilloché Dial

Our dial is cut on a hand-cranked rose engine from 1904, a machine so temperamental that only two people alive are permitted to touch it. The pattern — grain d'orge — catches the light in waves, so the face of the watch is never the same face twice.

Applied indices are gold, drawn wire, polished flat. The hands — dauphine, faceted, mirror-lapped — are set by feel, under breath.

CH. 04

The Oath of the Maison

We make fewer watches in a year than others make before lunch. Each leaves with a certificate signed twice — once in ink, once in person, across the workbench where it was born.

Set the hands above to your own hour. The watch is already running; it has been running since you arrived. When you leave, it will forget you politely, as all good instruments do.
