TITLE: LE CINÉMA REX
DESCRIPTION: A vintage 1927 single-screen picture palace rendered as an interactive night facade. A border of chasing marquee bulbs frames an illuminated art-nouveau serif sign; below, a cream lithographed poster for "The Violin of Dusk" (moonburst, sun rays, an inline-SVG violin) sits beside the Tonight's Programme schedule. A velvet-lined stalls console shows a seat map where some seats are already taken; picking seats and pressing "Print Ticket" dispenses a cream cardboard ticket from a brass slot, which tears along a real punched perforation into a stub when clicked.
TECHNIQUES:
- Chasing marquee bulbs via staggered negative animation-delay on radial-gradient spheres
- Lithograph poster built from layered repeating-conic-gradient rays, radial-gradient moon, and inline SVG violin with f-holes
- Seat map generated from a seeded PRNG so "taken" seats look organically clustered toward the screen-centre rows
- Ticket perforation made of a repeat-y radial-gradient punch-hole strip between two dashed rules; tearing applies jagged polygon clip-paths plus opposed rotations to both halves
- WebAudio: oscillator blips for seat clicks, filtered white-noise buffer bursts for the printer roll and paper rip, and an optional drone of detuned triangles plus looped low-passed noise for the projector hum
INTERACTION:
- Click showtimes to change the marquee billing; click free seats (up to four) to select, taken seats shake and explain themselves
- Press "Print Ticket" and the stub rises from the brass slot with a printer-roll sound
- Click or press Enter on the ticket to rip it along the perforation; toggle "Projector Hum" in the footer for the machine drone
PROMPT:
Design a full-bleed dark night scene for a 1927 single-screen cinema called LE CINÉMA REX. Background is deep navy (#101823) with a fixed, faintly twinkling star field of tiny radial-gradient dots. At the top, a marquee: a rounded brass-bordered panel with inner gold hairline, containing two horizontal rows of thirteen bulbs. Each bulb is a small sphere (radial-gradient from cream through marquee gold #d4af37 to dark brass) that brightens and glows in a chasing pattern using animation-delay of minus 0.175 seconds per index; the bottom row runs counter-phase. Between the bulb rows sits the cinema name in small-caps Georgia, letterspaced 0.3em, gold with a soft glow. Under the marquee, a small brass-rail caption reads "Place des Lilas · Est. 1927 · Projected by carbon arc".

Below, a two-column editorial block (collapsing to one column on small screens). Left: a cream poster with a double crimson keyline frame, filled with repeating-conic sun rays rising from the bottom, a glowing gold moon disc, and a hand-drawn inline SVG violin with scroll, body, f-holes and bridge. Poster copy: kicker "THE REX PRESENTS", stacked small-caps title "THE / VIOLIN / OF DUSK" with the middle word large and italic, a rule, the subtitle "A Romance in Six Reels", small-caps credits, and a mono line "1927 · Certificate U · 104 Minutes". Right: the programme list — three showtimes (17:00 Matinée 9d, 20:30 Gala 1s 6d, 23:00 Late 1s) as full-width buttons with mono hours in gold, italic descriptions, dashed separators, and hover states that slide them right with a gold left border; beneath, an italic house note and a mono list of house rules.

Next, the stalls console: a dark panel headed by a glowing cream screen arc and the label "THE SCREEN", then a crimson velvet tray (layered satin sheen over a crimson gradient) holding a seat map of rows A–K (skipping I), 8–14 seats each, with center aisles. Free seats are rounded velvet-red buttons with number labels that lift and brighten on hover; chosen seats turn marquee gold and glow; taken seats are dark with X hatching and disabled. A legend and a mono price line ("Admission 1s 6d each · Total …") sit under the map beside a gold "PRINT TICKET" button. Selection plays a short square-wave clack; exceeding four seats or clicking taken seats plays a head-shake animation and shows an italic gold toast.

At the bottom, the ticket tray: a horizontal brass slot, and beneath it the ticket — a cream cardboard strip with rounded left corners, containing mono brand line, small-caps crimson film title over a rule, row and seat line, time/price line, a pseudo-random serial, and a CSS barcode of stacked repeating-linear-gradients; then an 11px perforation strip (repeat-y radial-gradient punch holes with dashed edges), then the "ADMIT ONE" stub in vertical writing-mode. Printing deals the ticket downward from the slot with a noise-burst roll sound and a chime. Clicking the ticket tears it: both halves receive jagged sawtooth polygon clip-paths, the stub slides right and rotates 9 degrees, the main half counter-rotates, a filtered white-noise rip plays, and an italic note appears. On narrow screens the ticket stacks vertically and the perforation runs horizontally. Footer offers a "PROJECTOR HUM" pill toggle that starts a user-initiated WebAudio drone (50 Hz triangle plus 100 Hz sine, looped low-passed noise, slow LFO swell). All copy is period British pre-decimal, escaped properly, with custom gold selection color, brass scrollbar, and prefers-reduced-motion support that stills the bulbs and animations.
