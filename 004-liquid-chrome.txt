TITLE: Liquid Chrome
DESCRIPTION: A dark gallery room where eleven bodies of mercury float as true metaballs — a per-pixel threshold field shaded like polished metal, reflecting an animated environment of silver and steel bands with thin iridescent films of blue and rose. Blobs merge and split organically, are attracted toward the cursor, flinch away when struck, and a viscosity slider changes how thick and cohesive the metal feels. Above the surface hangs a giant metallic serif wordmark, "Liquid CHROME", distorted by an animated SVG ripple. Gallery furniture — exhibit plates, a vertical wall caption, a glass console — frames the piece like a museum installation.
TECHNIQUES:
- Real metaball field: a low-resolution grid accumulates r²/d² contributions from eleven blobs, thresholded at ~1.0 with a smoothstep band for antialiased edges
- Fake chrome shading from the field gradient: per-pixel normals drive a vertical environment function (silver to steel to deep bands, drifting over time) plus thin iridescent films (#8ab4f8, #f0b6c9), a 34-power specular from an upper-left light, and a fresnel rim
- Field rendered to an offscreen ImageData at grid resolution then upscaled with smoothing for soft, liquid edges at 60fps
- Physics: slow lissajous home orbits, cursor attraction with distance falloff, click impulses radiating outward, pairwise soft separation, per-frame damping tied to viscosity
- Metallic gradient text via background-clip:text on the wordmark, distorted by an inline SVG feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap ripple filter with a slowly animating baseFrequency
- Glass console with custom-styled range input whose thumb is itself a little chrome sphere
INTERACTION:
- Moving the cursor attracts nearby blobs like a stirring rod — a custom ring cursor follows the hand and tightens when pressed; blobs reach, merge around, and trail the pointer
- Clicking anywhere agitates the metal: blobs receive radial impulses away from the strike while rose and blue rings ripple outward across the surface
- The viscosity slider (0.00–1.00 poise) morphs the physics live: low values run fast and splattery with weak home pull, high values move slowly, merge stubbornly and cling to their orbits
- The wordmark ripples continuously; on touch devices a crosshair cursor replaces the rod and taps still agitate
PROMPT:
Build a single-file HTML page titled "LIQUID CHROME" — a dark gallery installation. Base color #05060a; chrome gradient stops from #f7fafd through #c9d1d9 to #697386 and a deep #262c38; iridescent accents #8ab4f8 and #f0b6c9 used sparingly at roughly 10–20% strength; text silver. Typography: the wordmark is huge ui-serif (clamp 3.4rem–12.5rem) with the word "Liquid" in italic swash style and "CHROME" in roman caps, filled with a vertical metallic gradient via background-clip:text and a drop shadow; captions and labels are ui-monospace, 10–12px, 0.3em letter-spacing, uppercase.

The entire viewport is a canvas where eleven mercury blobs live. Implement them as genuine metaballs: a coarse grid (cell ≈ min(viewport)/105 px) sums each blob's r²/d², threshold at 0.82–1.06 with smoothstep antialiasing, then shade inside pixels using the field gradient as a surface normal. The shading environment is a vertical chrome gradient (bright silver top, steel middle, deep bottom) whose bands slowly drift and ripple over time, crossed by two thin iridescent films of #8ab4f8 near 36% height and #f0b6c9 near 62%; add a tight 34-power specular from an upper-left light and a fresnel rim so blob edges read as polished metal. Render the field into an offscreen ImageData and upscale with smoothing. Above and below the metal, page overlays sit on a radial vignette: a header with two mono exhibit plates ("The Gallery of 100 / Room 04 / Element 80 · Hg · 200.59 u" and "Slow metal in residence / Do not touch the exhibit — it touches back"), a vertical-rl rotated caption on the left wall, a centered over-title "NOW SHOWING", the wordmark, an italic serif sub-copy inviting the visitor to stir and strike the metal, and a bottom hint line "MOVE TO ATTRACT · CLICK TO AGITATE · DRAG THE SLIDER TO THICKEN TIME".

Signature moment: the metal is alive. Blobs drift on slow lissajous home orbits, breathing slightly in radius. The cursor acts as a stirring rod — a 46px ring cursor (hidden native cursor) with a center dot follows the pointer, and blobs within roughly half the viewport are pulled toward it with distance falloff, merging into trails around the hand. Clicking applies outward impulses (stronger when close) plus expanding rose/blue rings drawn on the canvas. A glass console bar (blur, 1px silver border, inner top highlight) holds the viscosity slider: a custom range input with a chrome-sphere thumb and a gradient track, labeled VISCOSITY, whose value reads out as "0.62 poise". Viscosity scales damping (runny 0.90 to thick 0.98 per 60Hz frame) and the home-orbit spring constant (1.4 to 8.4), and slightly weakens cursor pull — so the metal's whole personality changes from splatter to slow honey.

Details: SVG filter for the wordmark uses feTurbulence (baseFrequency ~0.012 0.05, animated over 24s) into feDisplacementMap at scale 10 — omit gracefully if reduced motion. Custom selection in translucent blue; hidden scrollbars; focus-visible outlines. Responsive: console collapses to a centered column under 720px, wall caption and right plate hide on phones, touch devices get a crosshair and tap-to-agitate. prefers-reduced-motion renders one pre-composed still life of the field (no rAF loop), disables the ripple animation and rod easing, and re-renders a single frame when the slider moves. Zero network, zero images — everything is canvas math, gradients and one inline SVG filter.
