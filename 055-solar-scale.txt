TITLE: TRUE SCALE
DESCRIPTION: A true-scale solar-system explorer rendered edge-on as a single hairline against a starfield. A log-depth zoom slider (and pinch, wheel, and drag) travels from the Sun's roiling photosphere out to Neptune's orbit at 30 AU; every body is drawn at honest relative scale, which means the lesson arrives quickly: the solar system is almost entirely empty. Amber mono readouts report the current view width in kilometres, AU, and how long light takes to cross it; a serif italic interstitial narrates each regime of the journey ("From here, every human event fits inside a pixel of light four minutes old"); a data plaque slides in with true figures whenever a body fills a sensible share of the screen; and a compare mode lines up any two bodies on one scale with a ratio verdict.
TECHNIQUES:
- Logarithmic camera model: view-span interpolated exponentially between 3 million km and 10.5 billion km, so the zoom feels even from photosphere to heliosphere
- Canvas 2D orrery with true radii and orbital distances (Mercury 0.38 Earth-radii at 0.39 AU through Neptune 3.86 at 30.07 AU), radial-gradient shading, banded gas giants, ring ellipses, and pinprick fallbacks below half a pixel
- Star parallax tied to log-zoom with per-star twinkle phases, AU tick marks on the ecliptic hairline, and a pointer-anchored wheel zoom that keeps the km under the cursor fixed
- DOM/canvas hybrid UI: plaque data table swapped per focus, compare panel with live ratio verdicts, and eight regime interstitials cross-faded on span thresholds
INTERACTION:
- Drag the canvas to pan (free camera with km-from-Sol readout), scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click near a body to snap-focus it; keyboard +/− zoom, 0 returns to the Sun
- The zoom slider and body chips drive the same camera; the slider ticks and the amber diamond thumb reflect the log position
- Compare mode overlays two bodies at one true scale with their diameters and a ×-ratio verdict; the plaque auto-shows the dominant body when zoomed close
