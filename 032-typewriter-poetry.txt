TITLE: Poems for Strangers
DESCRIPTION: An intimate typewriter station set in a warm lamplit study. A three-quarter machine of glossy black round keys sits on a walnut-toned desk wall; a cream sheet is rolled in the carriage window under a red-tipped position guide, with a black-and-red ribbon band across the platen roller. A tilted muse note is clipped to the machine body showing the current prompt. To the right, a shelf of serif prompt cards deals questions like "write about the last door you opened", and a side pocket collects finished poems as a fanned stack of tiny folded sheets that can be clicked back into the carriage. Every keypress prints a single striking glyph with a synthesized clack; Enter rings a real bell, rattles the carriage and resets; Ctrl+Enter files the page with a paper swoosh.
TECHNIQUES:
- DOM-per-glyph typewriter engine: each character is a span with a per-key strike animation, word-wrap moves trailing glyph spans to the next line, and backspace erases spans individually
- Fully synthesized WebAudio: filtered-noise bandpass clacks with pitch variation, a three-partial sine bell for the margin ding, a four-step ratchet for carriage return, and a looped-noise lowpass sweep for filing
- Hand-drawn machine chrome in CSS only: radial-gradient glossy keys with 3D travel shadows, platen roller with end knobs, ruler with repeating-tick background, ribbon band split black/red
- Canvas dust motes drifting upward through the lamp glow with sine twinkle, paused when the tab hides; 3D card-flip prompt deck; flying-sheet filing animation from carriage to pocket
INTERACTION:
- Type on your real keyboard or click the on-machine keys — each press prints with a clack; Enter rings the bell and resets the carriage; Backspace lifts one letter; Ctrl+Enter files the poem into the side pocket
- Draw prompt cards (they flip in 3D), pin one to the machine as your muse, click a folded pocket sheet to reload it into the carriage (an unfinished page is tucked away first)
- Toggle the red/black ribbon and the machine's voice; margin bell warns six spaces early; counters track lines, words, keys struck and poems filed
