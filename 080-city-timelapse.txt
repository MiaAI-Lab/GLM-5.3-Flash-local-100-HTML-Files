TITLE: Rush Hour
DESCRIPTION: A city intersection — 5th & Meridian — rendered as a living long-exposure photograph. Vehicles stream through the crossing in four lanes each way, stamping red taillight, white headlight and amber-taxi glows onto a persistent trail plate that fades at a rate set by the exposure slider: low shutter lag builds silky ribbons of light, high shutter lag keeps crisp streaks. Around the crossing, four blocks of procedural buildings darken and ignite window by window as the sky cycles from midnight through dawn, noon, dusk and back; street lamps and traffic signals wake as daylight drains; a sun or moon crosses the sky and stars return. A full camera rig frames it all: blinking REC, live ISO and f-stop readouts that chase the daylight, frame and trail counters, a day-cycle scrubber with a gradient sky track, develop-a-frame freeze, and a fresh-plate reset.
TECHNIQUES:
- Long-exposure simulation: vehicle head/tail glows stamped into a half-resolution additive trail buffer that is faded per-frame with destination-out at an exposure-controlled rate, then composited over the scene — trails accumulate into continuous light streams
- Keyframed sky engine: nine color keyframes across 1440 minutes with a daylight() luminance function driving window glow, lamp bloom, vehicle-body visibility, star alpha and ISO readout
- Procedural city: seeded blocks of buildings with individually phased windows that ignite as darkness falls; street-lamp poles and signals drawn to a static road layer; sun/moon arcs with radial bloom
- Camera OSD: tabular-numeral mono readouts (clock, ISO, f-stop, exposure seconds, frame count, traffic state GRIDLOCK/RUSH/FLOWING), blinking REC dot, viewfinder corners and crosshair
- Traffic sim: spawn rate keyed to rush-hour windows, four-lane bidirectional flow, soft speed modulation, capped at 46 vehicles; letterboxed virtual 1600x1000 scene scaled to any viewport
- Film treatment: radial vignette, additive amber wash at night, a brightening "developed" flash on frame freeze; full reduced-motion and tab-hide handling
INTERACTION:
- Drag the day-cycle scrubber to jump to any minute — dawn ignition, noon glare, dusk trails, midnight stars — the whole scene re-renders instantly
- Press RUN THE DAY to let time flow (a full day in about ten seconds); press again to pause; the REC dot mirrors the state
- DEVELOP A FRAME freezes the moment and lifts it like a print from the developer bath
- Fresh plate wipes the trail buffer and traffic for a clean exposure
- The exposure slider changes how long light lingers on the plate — from silk-smooth rivers to crisp individual streaks — with a live shutter-lag word and camera readouts

The scene: a virtual 1600x1000 intersection — a horizontal and a vertical four-lane road with dashed centerlines, zebra crosswalks, and street lamps — crossed by streams of vehicles. Each vehicle stamps two radial glows (headlight and taillight, colored red/white/amber by direction and dice) into a persistent half-resolution additive buffer; the buffer fades each frame by an exposure-derived rate, so low exposure weaves continuous rivers of light and high exposure keeps short bright streaks. During daylight, faint car bodies appear over the trails.

Around it: four blocks of procedural buildings whose windows ignite one by one as darkness falls (warm gold or cool blue, each on its own sine phase), a sun that arcs across the sky by time of day with radial bloom, a moon and ninety seeded stars at night, street lamps that bloom amber only in darkness, and two traffic signals alternating north-south / east-west green on a one-minute phase.
