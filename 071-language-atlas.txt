TITLE: Tongues of Earth
DESCRIPTION: A typographic atlas of the great language families rendered as living dot-density cartography on aged atlas paper. A hand-engraved coastline survey (stylised point-in-polygon continents with inland water holes) is filled with thousands of jittered dots, each coloured by its language family via a nearest-hearth Voronoi settlement, so the world reads as soft watercolour continents of madder red, indigo slate, olive, ochre and plum. An ornamental double-ruled cartouche carries the title in letterspaced serif caps with plate number and star corners; italic Latin sea names and a fine graticule complete the engraving. Hovering any cluster makes its dots swell and ripple outward like a heartbeat while other families recede; selecting a family opens a specimen plaque whose enormous single glyph (ॐ, 水, ض, ந, ŋ...) becomes the page's centrepiece, with animated speaker counts, hearth coordinates and an italic provenance note.
TECHNIQUES:
- Canvas 2D dot-density world map: jittered grid survey tested against hand-authored lon/lat coastline polygons and lake holes, one batched path-fill per family for 60fps
- Nearest-hearth Voronoi assignment (~70 seed points) to colour the dot field by language family
- Pre-rendered offscreen "engraving layer" (graticule, coast strokes, land wash, italic sea labels) composited under animated dots each frame
- Eased per-family intensity with a distance-based sine wave radiating from the cursor for the heartbeat swell; other families fade back
- Ocean click ripples drawn as fading concentric rings; live mono coordinate readout via inverse projection
- Editorial print styling: double-rule cartouche with rotated-square diamond, SVG star corners and compass rose, CSS grain and vignette overlays, tabular-nums stat grid, custom selection and scrollbar colours
- Animated count-up of the speaker statistic with cubic ease-out; full prefers-reduced-motion static fallback
INTERACTION:
- Hovering the map swells the dots of the dominant family under the cursor like a heartbeat while a chip names the family and the coordinate readout tracks latitude and longitude
- Clicking a land cluster opens that family's specimen plaque; clicking open water drops an ink ripple
- The legend chips highlight on hover and keyboard focus, and toggle the plaque open or closed
- The plaque closes via its button or the Escape key; the speaker figure counts up on each open
- Everything is draggable-free but fully pointer- and keyboard-accessible, responsive from 360px to 4K, with a calm static render under reduced motion
PROMPT:
Create a single self-contained HTML page titled "Tongues of Earth — A Typographic Atlas of Language Families". The mood is a nineteenth-century engraver's atlas plate re-drawn with a living dot matrix. Palette: atlas paper #f0e9d8, ink #2f2a22, ocean wash #cdd9d4, and sixteen muted family colours (madder red #a4452f for Indo-European, indigo slate #3f5d7a for Sino-Tibetan, olive #6d7d4f for Niger-Congo, ochre #b08a3e for Afro-Asiatic, plum #7a5f8f for Austronesian, teal-slate #4a6d6d for Dravidian, and so on). Typography: an Optima/Palatine display serif with wide letterspacing for the cartouche title, italic Georgia for subtitles and sea names, and ui-monospace for plate numbers, coordinates and statistics.

Layout: a centred cartouche header ("TONGUES OF EARTH", subtitle "A typographic atlas of the great language families — every dot, three million voices", plate row "LINGVARUM TABULA · PLATE No. XXII · MMXXVI") framed by a double rule with diamond finial and four SVG star corners. Below, a full-width bordered map stage with an ocean gradient, graticule, canvas-drawn coastlines of stylised continents (North and South America, Greenland, Africa, one long Eurasian coast, Japan arc, Madagascar, the Malay archipelago, New Guinea, New Zealand, Britain and Ireland, Iceland, Cuba) and inland water holes for the Baltic, Black and Caspian Seas, the Persian Gulf, the Great Lakes and Hudson Bay. Italic canvas-set sea labels read OCEANUS ATLANTICUS, OCEANUS INDICUS, MARE PACIFICUM, MARE ARCTICUM. A compass rose SVG sits in the top-right corner; a mono coordinate readout sits bottom-left. Under the stage, a wrapping legend of sixteen small-caps chips, each with a colour swatch and speaker total; beneath that, a mono caption line. A vignette and a fine dot-grain overlay give the paper tooth.

The map is a canvas: a jittered grid of dots is tested against the polygons (point-in-polygon), inland lakes punch holes, and each surviving dot is assigned to the nearest of about seventy language-family hearth seeds (Voronoi), producing believable colour regions — red across Europe, Siberia and the Americas' colonial belts; indigo across China; ochre across the Arabic belt and North Africa; olive across sub-Saharan Africa; plum across the maritime world from Madagascar to Polynesia. Draw coastlines and graticule once to an offscreen layer; each frame composite that layer, then fill all dots family-by-family in a single batched path per family.

Signature moment: hovering a cluster makes that family's dots swell rhythmically — a sine wave radiating from the cursor so the dot field pulses like a heartbeat — while every other family recedes; the hovered family's name appears in a small chip with its colour. Clicking a cluster opens the specimen plaque: a fixed, double-ruled paper card sliding up from the lower right, with a colour bar in the family hue. Its left column holds one enormous glyph (ॐ for Indo-European, 水 for Sino-Tibetan, ض for Afro-Asiatic, ந for Dravidian, ŋ for Trans-New Guinea, Ö for Turkic, Ä for Uralic, Ñ for the Americas, and so on) labelled with its script, springing up with an overshoot ease. The right column carries the family name in spaced caps, a tabular stat grid (SPEAKERS counting up to e.g. 3,200,000,000; LIVING LANGUAGES; HEARTH with mono coordinates such as "45.0° N · 34.0° E — Pontic steppe"), bordered branch tokens (English · Hindi · Spanish · Bengali...), and an italic provenance line ("From the Pontic steppe to every harbour on Earth — the tongue that learned to sail."). Escape or the ✕ button closes it. Clicking open water drops a fading double-ring ripple.

All CSS in one style block, all JS in one script block, no external resources, system fonts only, custom ::selection and scrollbar colours, responsive from 360px (plaque becomes a full-width bottom sheet, legend wraps, stage shrinks) to 4K via clamp(), pointer events for hover, devicePixelRatio-aware canvas capped at 2x, a single requestAnimationFrame loop that pauses when the tab hides, and a prefers-reduced-motion path that renders a calm static frame with instant counts and no waves.
