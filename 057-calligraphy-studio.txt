TITLE: THE SCRIPTORIUM
DESCRIPTION: A calligraphy practice room on a dark wooden desk. A sheet of laid paper — ruled lines, a vertical chain line, an oxblood margin — is the drawing surface. The brush is velocity-weighted: slow strokes pool the ink and fatten the line, fast strokes run dry, fray into strands and rasp; the ink load visibly depletes with every metre of stroke until you dip the nib again. A ghost-stroke tracing mode renders the model glyph (A g R & Q f Z) through the paper, scores every stroke for adherence and coverage, and stamps a red seal with a grade word (awkward → graceful) onto the corner of the sheet. Nibs in three widths, undo, fresh sheet, an optional nib-scratch ambience, and a "master hand" grade that rises with honest practice.
TECHNIQUES:
- Pressure-by-velocity brush dynamics: an EMA of pointer speed maps to stroke width (1.5× nib at rest down to 0.42× at speed), with ink alpha coupled to reservoir level and a dry-brush mode (three jittered hairline strands) above the speed threshold or below 18% ink
- Ink pooling: slow movement (< 0.14 px/ms) drops radial-gradient blots under the line; the reservoir depletes by distance and gates drawing entirely when empty
- Ghost tracing and scoring: the model glyph rendered as faint fill plus oxblood outline on a canvas under the ink; an offscreen mask of the same glyph measures adherence (sampled stroke points on-mask) and coverage (glyph grid points hit by a wide stroke on a coverage canvas), summed into a 0–100 seal score
- Environment: laid paper via layered repeating-linear-gradients with a red margin rule, wood-plank desk background, incremental stroke rendering recorded for rescale-stable undo/redraw
INTERACTION:
- Draw with mouse, pen or finger (pointer capture, touch-action none); width, dryness and pooling all follow your speed in real time, and the ink bar falls as you write
- Dip refreshes the reservoir; Undo (or Ctrl/Cmd+Z) removes the last stroke; Fresh sheet clears; nib buttons change the base width; glyph buttons change the model
- Tracing mode toggles the ghost and scores each finished stroke, stamping the red seal with the numeric score and verdict; the nib-scratch toggle enables a bandpassed noise loop whose gain follows stroke speed
