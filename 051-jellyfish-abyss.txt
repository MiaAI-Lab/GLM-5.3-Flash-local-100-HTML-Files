TITLE: MEDUSA
DESCRIPTION: A full-bleed canvas scene of a deep-sea jellyfish ballet. The visitor scrolls to descend from the sunlit zone to a 2,400-metre abyss: god rays fade out, the water gradient darkens from twilight blue to near-black, and the cast of bioluminescent jellyfish grows from five to eighteen. Each jellyfish is a procedural canvas creature — a bezier bell that contracts and relaxes with a visible thrust pulse, trailing verlet-simulated tentacle ribbons and ruffled oral arms. A mono depth readout with pressure and temperature sits top-left, a specimen nameplate in italic serif swaps between four species bands, and a slim vertical gauge marks the ocean zones. Clicking the water fires an expanding ring of light that the curious jellyfish gather around; hovering the cursor draws them near.
TECHNIQUES:
- Canvas 2D bezier bell deformation driven by a sine contraction phase, with thrust applied only on the expanding half of the pulse
- Verlet-style chain simulation for tentacles and oral arms, swayed by per-segment sinusoids and smoothed with an exponential follower
- Scroll-mapped depth model that interpolates the water gradient, ray visibility, temperature and pressure readouts
- Additive-composite radial glows for bioluminescence, marine snow with parallax depth layers, and a species manager that spawns and cross-fades creatures per depth band
INTERACTION:
- Scrolling descends the water column: the palette darkens, god rays die out, and new species drift in as the cast grows
- Moving the pointer makes nearby jellyfish turn and drift toward it; clicking anywhere pulses a ring of cyan light they gather around
- The sound toggle synthesizes a two-tone abyssal drone with WebAudio (user-initiated) and adds a sonar-like ping to every light pulse
