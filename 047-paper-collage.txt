TITLE: TORN & ASSEMBLED
DESCRIPTION: A mixed-media collage wall on kraft corkboard. Sixteen scraps — painted canvas bits disguised as a store receipt, an addressed envelope, graph paper with a red plotted curve, a newspaper headline, a photo-booth strip, neon snips, magazine ransom letters A/N/X/O/!, a thread card, taped and stapled variants — each drawn with torn polygon edges, tape strips and staples, scattered and rotated. Everything is draggable with a z-jump on grab and a tape-wobble on release; a cobalt thread is hand-stitched through five pinned scraps with a dangling needle, and a Shuffle button recomposes the whole wall from the same pieces.
TECHNIQUES:
- Torn edges: seeded procedural SVG-style quadratic paths (jagged per-side teeth) applied as CSS clip-path on paper-textured divs
- Every scrap's "print" is a hand-coded Canvas 2D painter (receipt ledger rows, envelope ruling, graph grid + red sine plot, headline rules, four-frame photo-booth silhouettes, neon wedges, ransom letters in rotating font/fill pairs)
- Tape strips and staples as clipped divs with slight rotations; long soft drop-shadows that deepen when lifted
- Cobalt thread: live canvas overlay drawing quadratic curves between anchor scraps with sway, sag and a trailing needle, redrawn every frame
- Ransom-note masthead built from individually styled spans with nth-child font/fill/rotation variation
INTERACTION:
- Drag any scrap (pointer capture); it jumps to the top of the pile with a lifted shadow and wobbles on release like complaining tape
- Keyboard: arrow keys nudge (shift for coarse), Enter/Space rotates a scrap
- "Shuffle the Wall" reseeds every position and rotation at once; "Tidy Grid" snaps all scraps into an orderly grid (and back); "Thread: On/Off" hides the stitching
PROMPT:
Design TORN & ASSEMBLED: a corkboard collage wall on warm kraft (#c9a875) with speckle dots, diagonal weave texture, cross-corner room light, and inset edge shadows. The masthead is a ransom-note title "TORN & ASSEMBLED" — every letter an individually colored, differently-fonted, slightly rotated tile (cream monospace, neon pink sans, dark chip with lime italic serif, newsprint serif in cobalt), above an italic sub about cut, torn or stolen materials. Three tape-chip buttons (jagged clip-path polygons, mono uppercase): "Shuffle the Wall", "Tidy Grid: Off", "Thread: On".

The wall itself is a large textured kraft panel (min-height ~820px) with faint pencil ruled-guides overlay. On it, sixteen absolutely-positioned scraps: a torn store receipt (Courier ledger rows, red italic "paid in coin" stamp), a lined envelope with a cobalt wax-seal "3", graph paper with green grid and a crimson plotted curve labeled "fig. 7 — the argument", a clipped newspaper headline "DUSK COMES EARLY — column 4, page 11, cut crooked", a 4-frame photo-booth strip of dark silhouette heads with flash glare, neon pink/cyan snips, four big ransom letters (A, N, X, O, !) each with random background/font pairings, a thread card ("cobalt, no. 12 — keep"), a taped caption card ("the good tape — do not use for parcels") with two rotated tape strips, and a stapled headline ("found under the mat") with a steel staple. Torn edges come from seeded jagged quadratic clip-paths; all faces are Canvas 2D painters; shadows are long and soft.

Signature interactions: grabbing any scrap raises it to the top of the pile with a deeper drop shadow and a slight rotation wobble that decays like settling tape; a single cobalt thread (#2b44ff, 1.6px, sagging quadratics that sway gently and end in a needle) stitches through five anchor scraps on a live canvas overlay, following drags in real time. "Shuffle" reseeds all positions/rotations in one motion with wobbling settles; "Tidy Grid" snaps everything into a strict grid (the caption deadpans "tidied — it will not last"). Typewritten captions live on some scraps; a mono caption bar under the wall narrates ("piece 7 in motion · thread follows"), and arrow keys nudge pieces for keyboard users. Reduce-motion disables wobble and thread animation but keeps the thread drawn. The whole page should feel like a friend's studio wall — playful, physical, slightly too honest.
