TITLE: TREMOR
DESCRIPTION: A control-room seismograph station on dark engraved panels. A wide drum window shows cream smoke-paper with red hairline grid scrolling leftward; an ink needle draws your pointer's restlessness onto the paper with amplitude decay, line thickness driven by pointer pressure and excitement. Below, a console row holds a Richter-style gauge with a live needle, a scrolling event log with timestamped magnitude entries, and an operator console with sensitivity and paper-speed sliders, an armable alarm strip, a clear-drum button and a replayable archive macro-quake. The room lamp shifts green to amber to blinking alarm red as tremors build and decay.
TECHNIQUES:
- Ring-buffer trace rendering: pointer velocity pumps an excitation energy variable with exponential decay; the trace is summed sines plus jitter, amplitude-clamped at the paper rails, drawn as width-banded polyline segments with pressure-scaled stroke width
- Scrolling grid rendered in canvas coordinates tied to the sample cursor so hairlines move with the paper; a drawn stylus arm with pivot housing, counterweight tail and pen tip rides the newest sample
- Richter estimate via log10 mapping of peak trace amplitude to M 0.8-9.7, feeding an SVG gauge needle, magnitude word and naturalist commentary
- State-lamp with three states, timestamped event log with strength classes, brown-noise lowpass rumble synthesis with gain scheduling, alarm beacon with blink animation
INTERACTION:
- Move or shake your pointer anywhere on the page — velocity pumps the needle, pressure and excitement thicken the ink line, and quakes above threshold are auto-detected, logged with time and magnitude, and toasted
- Sliders tune sensitivity (x0.4-3) and paper speed (40-360 mm/min, which truly changes sampling rate); Arm alarm adds synthesized rumble during quakes and a chime plus beacon flash on completion; Clear drum wipes the record
- Replay quake re-executes a stored 1952 macro-quake (foreshock, mainshock, aftershocks) built from gaussian energy packets; the gauge, log and drum record it identically to a live one
